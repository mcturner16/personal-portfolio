Part 1 - Variations of the same Graph

Here I have a collection of my data analytics work which I will build and continue to add on to this Portfolio. Starting with some simple Vega-Lite practice.

This first chart takes a Movies Dataset from the Kaggle User SibamSamanta07 which contains metadata about 45,466 Movies. This chart looks at the median revenue in USD for each genre of Movies.

This second chart looks at the average revenue for each genre instead of the median. You can see how the rankings and the x-axis shifts.

For example, the median revenue for a Horror Movie in this dataset is $25,519,166, ranking 11th out of 20 Genres. Whereas the average revenue for a Horror Movie is more than double the median, at $53,896,875. However, this ranks only 16th out of the 20 Genres. Meanwhile the X-Axis roughly doubles in range compared to the first graph, but looks visually similar to the naked eye.

For the third chart, I decided to go back to the median but instead find the median "profit", defined as revenue minus budget.

You can see that Animation movies go from 6th in median and average revenue to 1st in median profit. Family movies go from 4th in median revenue and 5th in average revenue to 2nd in median profit. This is because of far lower budgets on average and median than the genres ranked above them in revenue - Adventure, Fantasy, Action and Science Fiction. Interestingly, Foreign and Western Movies dipped into negative median profit.

For the fourth chart, I naturally decided to look at the average profit.

The Adventure Genre reclaimed its spot as having the highest average profit, with Fantasy 2nd. Family and Animation dropped to 3rd and 4th, respectively. This is likely due to major blockbuster movies creating a sort of boom or bust dynamic. With the major hits dragging the average up, but the median staying lower due to less successful movies. The Adventure Genre's median profit is $54,269,486 and the average profit is $127,826,344.

Part 2 - Fixing a bad Visualization

Here we see a very misleading visualization published by the St. Louis Fed with the data sourced from the SIPRI Military Expenditure Database.

This post was made on January 22, 2023, showing the Top 6 Countries in the world by Military Spending. With the United States' budget being shown on the right Y-Axis, and the budgets of China, Russia, the United Kingdom, India and Saudi Arabia being shown on the left Y-Axis. This was obviously created as a scare tactic to make people look at it and think that China had surpassed the United States in Military Spending, when this is clearly not the case if you read closely.

With the data source taken directly from the SIPRI Database, I decided to update this graph to 2025, using 2024 constant dollars instead of 2020 constant. And I decided to stretch the timespan from 1990 to 2025. And as a result, with a single Y-Axis, the United States' Military Budget is shown to be far higher than 2nd Place China in reality to the naked eye. However, it does leave the other smaller countries at the bottom looking a bit cluttered.

Initially I decided to take the Top 10 Countries and aggregate the rest of the world into another category. However, I ultimately decided against this because having 10 lines made the bottom feel too cluttered, and the "Rest of World" Category would have itself been highly misleading because the dataset contained many null values that I had no way of knowing, and thus set to 0. For example - Do you really think that North Korea has a Military Budget of $0 in the year 2025? According to this graph, it would have, even if the underlying data was under the hood.

In order to better represent the differences in Military Spending of the other countries in the Top 6 without having two confusing Y-Axes, I decided to create a second graph excluding the United States itself, so that the Y-Axis can be shrunk down significantly.

Part 3 - Different interpretations of the same data.

Today, I will take a look at the adoption of Electric Vehicles in the United States, China and Worldwide. The data was compiled and sourced from the Ember Think Tank's free EV Sales Data Tracker.

The point of this exercise is to show how trends can be interpreted with long term and short term data.

This graph shows the percentage of vehicles sold that are Battery Electric or Plug-In Hybrid in each month beginning in January 2016. From this data you can see that the United States has undeniably become a laggard to the global trend, and that China (powered by its domestic car industry) has become a global leader in EV Adoption. While the United States is well below average in EV Adoption, possibly due to large ranges of car usage, lack of charging stations or cultural and political opposition - the long term graph still shows an upward trend despite the peak in September 2025 and then subsequent dip.

Note on Seasonality: The sharp, recurring dips visible in the graph every January and February in China are normal seasonal patterns due to tax breaks expiring yearly in December, combined with the Chinese New Year. You can read about this phenomenon being documented in the South China Morning Post.

If you shrink the scale to only beginning in January 2025 - You will see that the share of EVs being sold in the United States peaked in September 2025, and looks to be on a downward trend since then. This is due to the Trump Administration's decision to end the Electric Vehicle Tax Credit on September 30, 2025. If you look at this short term trend, you may come to the conclusion that the United States is a global laggard doomed to never mass adopt EVs, and rely on ICE (Internal Combustion Engine) Vehicles permanently. Indeed, the Big 3 Automakers posted a loss of over $52 Billion in write-downs, charges and cancelled supplier contracts. However, looking closely, you may note that the share of EVs has slowly begun its upward trend again in the United States from the nadir of November 2025.

I believe it is very easy to mislead readers with data visualizations by presenting the same data. As seen in this example where I showed the adoption of Electric Vehicles with the same dataset, while using different beginning points in order to show what look like different long term trends. And also seen in Part 2 of my work where I displayed an example of fixing a bad visualization.
